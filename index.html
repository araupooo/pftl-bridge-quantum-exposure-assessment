Bridge Quantum-Exposure and Migration Assessment

An assessment of the proposed cross-chain bridge that would move value between the native asset on the ledger and a wrapped representation on an external chain: its quantum custody risks, cross-chain replay exposure, and a migration path to hybrid ML-DSA / SLH-DSA signing.

Reproduction: python3 scripts/run_all.py from the pack root (Python 3 standard library only). Measured result: 7 of 7 checks PASS, 29 of 29 assertions PASS, 0 FAIL.

1. Summary

  1. The ledger side is quantum-safe by design. Accounts and validators sign with ML-DSA-65 from genesis, with domain-separated contexts that explicitly include bridge witnesses (whitepaper section 1.11).
  2. The quantum exposure is concentrated in exactly the places the whitepaper’s own scope limit names: bridge proofs wrap in Groth16 (pairing-based, classical), and external-chain contracts “use their own cryptography” (classical). A third exposure is inherited, not stated: bridge witness keys are secp256k1 or ed25519, both breakable by Shor’s algorithm.
  3. The logic defences are sound and measured. Quorum counting, claim-ID consumption, domain separation, duplicate and reorder handling are verified in the replay harness: 7 of 7 checks pass, 29 of 29 assertions pass. Reordered validations produce an identical final-state digest across 4 runs; duplicated validations are idempotent; a second mint of the same lock is rejected; tampered validations are rejected; the 24-of-35 quorum boundary is exact.
  4. The bridge premise itself is unverified. No primary source confirms an ERC-20 representation on Base, a Base bridge contract, or a genesis bridging procedure. The assessment treats the bridge as a stated premise and assesses the design that would follow from it; it does not assert the bridge exists.
  5. Migration is plannable now, but two exposures have no complete fix today: Groth16 bridge proofs and destination-chain cryptography.

3. Pinned architecture (summary)

Full detail: architecture.md.

  • Custody. Source-chain lock held by a door account or lock contract (mechanism confirmed from the inherited design; this bridge’s custodian unverified, A3).
  • Mint/burn authority. The destination contract mints only on a verified quorum of witness attestations; redemption burns and releases the lock (assumed, A3).
  • Quorum. 24 of 35 as stated in the whitepaper; bridge-witness application assumed (A4); validator count conflicted (A5).
  • Supply accounting. s_native + s_shielded + s_external + s_in-flight = s_authorized; a claim occupies exactly one category at each stage (whitepaper section 1.3). A double-mint breaks this identity by counting one claim twice.
  • Attestations. Witness keys are secp256k1 or ed25519; contexts are domain-separated, including for bridge witnesses; claim IDs are consumed on execution.
  • Scope limit. Bridge proofs wrap in Groth16; external-chain contracts use their own cryptography. The post-quantum claim covers account and validator authorisation and nothing broader.

5. Measured results

The replay harness (scripts/run_all.py, Python 3 standard library only) models bridge validations as signed attestation records and tests ordering, duplication, quorum, and tamper handling. Signatures in the model are HMAC-SHA256 tags with deterministically derived keys, explicitly labelled a LOGIC MODEL: they test the attestation logic, not cryptographic primitives.

6. Migration plan (summary)

Full detail: migration.md. ML-DSA-65 is the recommended bridge signature scheme (category 3, 1,952-byte public keys, 3,309-byte signatures) — the same parameter set the ledger already uses. ML-DSA-87 (category 5) is the higher-assurance option. SLH-DSA is the lattice-failure fallback, a poor fit for high-frequency attestation at 7,856–49,856-byte signatures.

There is no NIST hybrid-signature standard. The transition vehicle is the IETF LAMPS composite-ML-DSA draft (AND-mode verification, keys never reused standalone) — revision -19 expired 23 October 2026 and is not an RFC. Single ML-DSA in X.509 is standardised in RFC 9881 and is the end state. Transport hardens with ML-KEM-768 in a hybrid PQ/T construction per SP 800-227 section 4.6. Firmware/release signing uses LMS/XMSS under SP 800-208.

Phases: 0 inventory and crypto-agility; 1 hybrid composite witness signatures; 2 quorum migration with a dual-signature window; 3 classical deprecation with a versioned domain separator; 4 full ML-DSA operation and annual review. Rollback protection: versioned domains, no downgrade after cutover, claim-ID epoch continuity, emergency mint halt at 12 compromised keys (half the quorum margin).

7. Limitations

See limitations.md. In short: the bridge premise is unverified; no deployed bridge was available to verify; Groth16 bridge proofs and destination-chain cryptography have no complete fix today; residual double-spend paths (source-chain double recording, destination re-mint without a processed-claim set, source-chain reorganisation) are open; the harness is a logic model, not a cryptographic test.
